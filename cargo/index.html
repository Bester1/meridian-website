Conditions of carriage

These apply to every load we broker, domestic or cross-border. They are published because a condition you can read before you commit is worth more than one you discover afterwards.

The truck that arrives is the truck we vetted

Before a carrier is engaged we confirm CIPC registration, goods-in-transit cover verified with the insurer directly rather than from a certificate, operating licence disc, and a cross-border permit where the lane requires one.

Identity is checked at the gate, not on paper

At collection the driver's identification, licence and the vehicle and trailer registrations are photographed and matched against the booking. A registration that does not match stops the load. A substituted driver does not — that is usually a shift change — but it is confirmed with the carrier on the number we hold for them before the load is released, and who confirmed it is recorded.

No subcontracting without written consent

The carrier we confirm is the carrier that hauls. Passing a load on without our written agreement is a breach of the rate confirmation, and it is the single most common route by which freight is lost.

The rate is agreed in writing before the vehicle moves

One rate confirmation, held by both sides, stating the rate, the lane, the equipment, the dates and the payment term. Nothing is settled by telephone and confirmed later.

You are told where your freight is before you ask

Position confirmed at collection, in transit and ahead of delivery. A load running behind is reported by us, not discovered by you.

Proof of delivery on file the day it delivers

Captured at delivery and held against the load. Detention beyond the agreed appointment is claimed at the time, while it is still provable, rather than raised a month later when it is not.

Payment

Stated here rather than negotiated per load. A broker who cannot explain their terms plainly is a broker whose terms will move.

WhoTermWhy
Shippers, new account 50% on rate confirmation, balance on proof of delivery A first load between two parties who have not worked together carries risk on both sides. The deposit is released to the carrier, not held by us.
Shippers, established account 30 days from proof of delivery Available once the company is CIPC-verified and has run loads with us. Normal commercial terms for a normal commercial relationship.
Carriers, standard 30 days from proof of delivery Paid on the date stated in the rate confirmation. Not on the date the shipper pays us — that is our exposure to carry, not yours.
Carriers, early settlement Proof of delivery plus 48 hours, less 4% Optional, chosen by the carrier at booking and stated on the rate confirmation. Diesel and wages do not wait thirty days, and a carrier who would rather have the cash should be able to have it.

Straight talk

What a broker is worth

The commission pays for the check: that the operator is registered, insured and licensed, that the vehicle arriving is the one we engaged, and that somebody carries the consequence if it is not. Matching, paperwork and reporting are automated so that the check is where the time actually goes.

Typical brokerage on domestic lanes runs 5–10% of the freight rate; cross-border 10–15% — the range covers what can go wrong and the vetting that keeps it from going wrong.
