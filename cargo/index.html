The freight desk

What the desk covers

Southern African freight runs on WhatsApp — fast, familiar, and completely forgetful. We keep the speed and add the discipline: vetting, rate confirmations, tracked execution, POD on file, and a record that follows both parties.

Shippers

Post a load, get a vetted truck

Send the load once — lane, equipment, dates, commodity. You get back a matched carrier with the documents already checked: CIPC registration, goods-in-transit insurance, operating licence disc, references. Rate confirmation in writing before the truck rolls.

Carriers

Haul for a desk that pays

Clear loads with honest rates and payment terms stated up front. No last-minute rate games, no ghosting on invoices. Loads matched to your corridors and equipment — including cross-border work if your papers are in order.

Cross-border is our home ground: Beira and Maputo corridors out of Gauteng, the port runs from Durban, Walvis Bay up the west coast. Cross-border adds customs, permits and border-post timing — a desk that knows the document stack saves you nights at the border.

How every load runs

Load posted

One message: lane, equipment, dates, commodity, weight. We handle it from there.

Carrier matched & vetted

Corridor + equipment fit first; then the file check — CIPC, GIT insurance, OL disc, references. New carriers run supervised until proven.

Rate confirmed in writing

Both sides see the same rate confirmation before anything moves. Payment terms stated up front — 30 days from POD unless agreed otherwise.

Tracked transit

Status updates at pickup, mid-route and pre-delivery. If a load runs long, we're on it before you've noticed.

POD filed, detention claimed

Proof of delivery captured on the day, filed against the load. Detention over appointment time is auto-claimed — money that usually just evaporates.

Paid & recorded

Carrier paid on terms. Both sides rated. The next load starts with a record, not a promise.

Straight talk

What a broker is worth

A load board shows you a phone number. A group chat shows you whoever shouted loudest. Neither checks whether the truck is insured, licensed, or has ever completed a load like yours. That check is the job — and it's what the commission pays for. The rest — the speed, the matching, the paperwork — is what we automated so the desk can focus on it.

Typical brokerage on domestic lanes runs 5–10% of the freight rate; cross-border 10–15% — the range covers what can go wrong and the vetting that keeps it from going wrong.
